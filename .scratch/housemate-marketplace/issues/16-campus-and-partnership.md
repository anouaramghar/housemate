## Question

The country is decided. The campus is not, and neither is the thing that actually matters about it.

- **Which university?** A named one, not a profile.
- What makes a campus a good first target — housing scarcity, student numbers, an active existing
  housing group to displace, international-student share, a housing office that would plausibly
  engage?
- **Is the partnership actually reachable?** Do you have a real route into an institution — a
  contact, a student union, a society, a course — or is it aspirational?

The last point is load-bearing. If no partnership is reachable, constraint 13 has to be revisited and
this ticket should say so rather than papering over it. The fallback options from charting were
leading with the scam problem, coexisting inside the existing groups, or entering late at the
coordination step.

## What ticket 06 already fixed

Do not re-litigate these here:

- **The country is the UK.** Decided on joint-tenancy market shape plus Equality Act Sch. 5 para. 3.
- **The joint-tenancy filter is satisfied by definition** now that the country is UK — whole
  properties let to groups on joint and several liability is the norm nationally. The filter that
  eliminated most US cities does not discriminate between UK campuses, so it is no longer a
  selection criterion. Something else has to do the choosing.
- **The legal regime is fixed.** Campus choice no longer changes jurisdiction. It is now purely a
  go-to-market decision.

That last point is the reason this ticket no longer blocks the build. It blocks *launch*.

## What should decide it

With jurisdiction and market shape removed, the remaining criteria are all about density and access:

- ~~**A real route in.** Whose phone number do you have?~~ **No longer applicable** — there is no
  route into any institution, so this cannot discriminate between campuses. See
  [Resolution](#resolution-the-partnership-half).
- **Liquidity in one season.** The product is worthless below critical mass, and mass is per-campus,
  not national. A smaller university where you can reach a large share beats a larger one where you
  reach a rounding error.
- **International-student share.** The problem statement's sharpest persona — arriving without a
  friendship group already formed — concentrates here.
- **Housing scarcity.** Urgency drives adoption of a coordination tool. A comfortable market does not
  need one.
- **An incumbent to displace.** An active Facebook or WhatsApp housing group is evidence of demand
  and a distribution channel simultaneously.

## Resolution: the partnership half

**There is no route into an institution.** No contact, no student union, no society, no course. The
ticket warned that this point was load-bearing and that it should say so plainly rather than paper
over it. It says so.

**Constraint 13 is amended: the partnership comes off the critical path.** Distribution runs instead
through the **existing student housing groups** — the Facebook and WhatsApp groups that already exist
at every campus. A university partnership is pursued in parallel as upside, never as a precondition.

### Why the partnership was the wrong thing to depend on

**The clocks do not match.** A university takes a term or two to say yes. The housing season lasts
weeks. Depending on a housing office approving before September means **one attempt per year**, and
missing the window costs twelve months. That is an unacceptable dependency for a product that has not
yet proven anything.

### Why the existing groups are the better route

1. **The liquidity is already there.** Go to where the students are instead of hoping an institution
   delivers them.
2. **Nobody's permission is required.** No administrative delay, no external dependency, no gate.
3. **Those groups are the proof of the problem.** An 800-member WhatsApp group of students hunting
   for housemates *is* the pain this product addresses, and it is evidence the demand exists before a
   line of code is written.

### What this costs

The partnership was also the credibility story — an institution's endorsement answers "is this
legitimate?" for free. Entering through unofficial groups forfeits that, and the product has to earn
trust on its own, with verification as the only signal it can offer at launch. Accepted.

It also means launch quality depends entirely on execution inside those groups, which is
promotion work rather than product work, and is not something the spec covers.

## What still has to be decided: which city

With no contact to anchor the choice, the selection criteria collapse to observable facts, and the
question becomes researchable:

- **Which UK cities have the most active student housing groups** that can actually be joined?
- **Where is student housing scarcity worst?** Urgency drives adoption of a coordination tool; a
  comfortable market does not need one.
- **Where is the international-student share highest?** That is where the problem statement's
  sharpest persona concentrates.
- **Where is the ratio of students to available whole properties least favourable?**

Note that "a real route in" — previously the criterion that outranked all others — no longer applies,
since there is no route anywhere. Density and evidence now decide.

## Second-order

market. They are a competitor to the landlord packet, but possibly a **channel** into the
