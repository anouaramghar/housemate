## Question

Constraint 6 says a formed household hands the landlord a shareable group profile. Charting assumed
its job was to make strangers look **trustworthy**. The competitive research undercuts that: a
landlord's exposure under joint-and-several liability is **financial, not social**, and the market
already has a product for it — professional guarantor services like Housing Hand, which sign the
guarantor deed directly, claim ~98% landlord acceptance, and already hold university partnerships.

A packet that carries no money loses to one that does. So decide what the packet is really for:

- Is it **"this group is ready to transact"** — complete, documented, fast, no chasing five students
  for references? That's a speed-and-completeness claim, sits inside lease-coordination scope, and is
  honest about not carrying financial risk.
- Is it **"trust these strangers"**? If so, what does it offer that a guarantor doesn't, and why
  would a landlord weigh it?
- Is it **neither** — should the packet be dropped, and constraint 6 reduced to nothing more than a
  shareable group contact?
- Do you **integrate with** guarantor services rather than competing? That reframes them from
  incumbent to channel — they already sell to the same universities you plan to partner with.

Be honest about the consequence: if the packet is only a speed claim, it is worth materially less
than charting assumed, which feeds directly into
[Monetisation](11-monetisation-model.md).

Related: CoBuy's model suggests the **lock** should emit a co-created agreement artifact — plausibly
the honest core of what the packet actually is.
