# Monetisation — the landlord product, and the university tension

Type: grilling
Status: open
Blocked by: 06
Parent: [Student Housemate Marketplace](../map.md)

## Question

The model is: free for students, landlords pay once they become a side (constraint 11). That is a
direction, not a decision.

Decide:

- **What does a landlord actually pay for**, and at what moment — access to formed groups, listing
  placement, the group credibility packet, something else?
- Is it **per placement, subscription, or lead-based**?
- **What has to be true first?** Landlords pay for reliable supply of good tenants; how many formed
  households per season is that?
- Does charging landlords change **whose interests the product serves** — and would students notice?

Then resolve the tension charting surfaced. Distribution runs through a **university partnership**
(constraint 13), which puts institutional money back on the table after it was explicitly rejected
during charting. If the university is the distribution channel:

- Are they also a **customer**?
- Does taking their money change what the product is **allowed** to do — reporting, compliance,
  control over who gets matched with whom?
- Is a university that pays compatible with a reputation system it does not control?

Waits on [Which campus, and is a partnership actually reachable?](06-campus-and-partnership.md) —
whether a partnership exists at all decides whether this tension is real.

## Added after research

[Is group formation genuinely unserved?](03-competitive-reality-check.md) undercut the most obvious
revenue story before it was written down. Charting assumed landlords would pay for the **group
credibility packet**. But a landlord's exposure is financial, not social, and guarantor services
(Housing Hand and similar) already sell exactly that — signing the guarantor deed directly, with
~98% claimed acceptance. A packet carrying no money competes badly against one that does.

So resolve [What is the landlord packet actually for?](12-landlord-packet-purpose.md) before
answering this ticket. If the packet turns out to be only a **speed-and-completeness** claim, the
landlord-pays model is worth materially less than charting assumed, and the alternatives — students
paying, universities paying, or a guarantor-service partnership rather than competition — deserve a
real look rather than a reflexive rejection.

The research also confirmed the reverse: **do not monetise by taking the lease**. HomeShare, HubHaus,
Quarters, Common and The Collective all solved group formation by becoming the landlord, and all are
dead. That path is closed, and constraint 5 should be considered strengthened rather than merely
assumed.
